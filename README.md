# Polymath (Math Mage / Stats Sorcerer)

# My math Journey:

For as long as I can remember, I’ve always loved math and numbers. I always tried to recognize a pattern into everything. I love methodical steps to problems and writing.

My majors throughout my university experience explain my journey. I first started as a [MathPhys major](https://www.drps.ed.ac.uk/21-22/dpt/utmapyb.htm) at the [UoE](https://www.ed.ac.uk/) because I loved both subjects in [high school](https://windermerehs.ocps.net/). Once I took [MfP2](https://www.drps.ed.ac.uk/21-22/dpt/cxphys08036.htm), I realized that I particularly didn't like that area of physics (I squashed the diagrams that I drew). I then decided to try [applied math](https://catalog.usf.edu/preview_program.php?catoid=25&poid=13237) at USF because I struggled writing my own proofs (which is a decent chunk of [pure math](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_mathematics)). I realized I liked my stats courses more after taking [probability theory](https://catalog.usf.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=25&coid=114503) and [MathStats](https://catalog.usf.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=25&coid=114501). I then decided to do the [MAS program](https://science.psu.edu/stat/graduate-programs/master-of-applied-statistics) at PSU to learn about the applications of statistics to other subjects.

I’ve realized how much math and logic is behind the skills I learned too: 
- I think of [Excel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Excel) as one giant matrix from linear algebra
- [R](https:/en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RStudio) is a programming language designed specifically for statisticians
- Technical writing is very methodical in the sense that you first write code, get an output, and then interpret the results
- [Tableau](https://www.tableau.com/) can make beautiful dashboards with all of the data
- [Minitab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minitab) feels like Excel but specifically for statisticians.

I’ve also learned real-world applications to other subjects. In [time series](https://online.stat.psu.edu/stat510/), I can apply it to stock markets. 
For [data mining](https:/online.stat.psu.edu/stat508/), I can apply that to AI and ML algorithms. [Mathematical modeling](https://catalog.usf.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=25&coid=116846)
taught me you can’t accurately model all physical phenomena through math equations (there is always some error or epsilon term).

Taking courses in other disciplines was also interesting for me too. Like in [ISM 4641](https://catalog.usf.edu/preview_course_nopop.php?catoid=25&coid=116878), the course had lots of stats
behind the data analysis. Using [regression methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regression_analysis) to predict new values in business settings. 
My programming courses felt similar to [formal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic).

# My Math / Stats [Inflection Points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflection_point): 
# Calculus:

My first inflection point in math was when I took AP Calculus AB my junior year of high school. All of the previous math I learned just stuck together like glue. Algebra and geometry just made
sense although I already took courses on those subjects. I loved using the tools of all of the math I used throughout my life.

# Math Stats (the 3rd course in it):

[Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_statistics) (the intersection of math and statistics). This is the course where you get to derive probability distributions.

During the summer of 2026, I was taking a [mathematical statistics](https://online.stat.psu.edu/stat415/) course. I was stressing over the course but all that struggling in the end was worth
it. This made me realize I can study abstract concepts in math as well. All the math I learned throughout the life can be literally applied to everything in the universe. Some sort of
philosophical awakening to me. This made me reason everything with formal logic. This ultimately caused me to open up a LinkedIn and this website. I need to make connections with people and
much rather do it online. I like texting much more than talking to people in public (specifically, technical writing).
